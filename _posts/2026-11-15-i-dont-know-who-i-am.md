My whole life, people have called me talented. Multi-talented, actually, which sounds like a compliment until you realise it is also a very polite way of saying *she cannot pick one thing and stay there.*

I paint. I play bass. I play piano. I sing. I've directed films, shot campaigns, written songs, built apps. For years I wore that list like a medal. Then one day it started to feel less like a medal and more like a receipt: proof of all the things I started because my brain needed a new shiny object to chase.

> Turns out "you're so good at everything" and "you can't finish anything" can live in the same body. Rent-free. For thirty-odd years.

## The diagnosis

There's a specific kind of grief that comes with finding out your brain has been running a different operating system the whole time. You don't just mourn what was hard. You mourn the version of you who might have existed if someone had handed her the manual earlier.

I call this *analysing the multiverse*. I do it a lot.

## So, who am I?

Honestly? I don't know yet. But I'm trying to like her anyway. Quirks and all. Especially the quirks.
