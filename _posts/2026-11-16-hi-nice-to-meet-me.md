Hmm. This is going to be a long one. Got to start this off right.

The past two years have been the longest in my life. So much, and I mean _so much_ happened that I'm not really in the liberty to share but I have never been this emotionally and mentally overstimulated. Ever. And yes, I am known to be a bit of a drama mama but this time I really mean it.

The crazy part is that the lows were the lowest and the highs turned out to be lows too. Just the damnest of lows wrapped up in seismic, ground-rattling happiness. The layers come off and it turns out I don't even know what the fuck is going on nor do I know where/who the fuck I want to be.

I may have spoken about it briefly--and I know I joke about it a lot, it's a defence mechanism--but I officially got diagnosed neurodivergent August of 2025. And how did it come to that? 

Woof. Like I said, _so_ much happened. Ironically, they weren't happening directly to me, it was me-adjacent, but boy did it do a number on me. So much so that I was convinced I was developing chronic anxiety. When I was 19, I was diagnosed with GAD, which gave me the ticket to "explaining" why I was such an anxious person, and I thought "Well, alright then. It is what I'm told it is." But during last year's saga of shit hitting the fan, the physical reaction I was having to it, the discomfort, I had to see a doctor again. I needed to have a plan, whether it be more therapy or maybe officially start medication, I didn't care, I could NOT be the anxious wreck I was everyday, not with Jolie always with me. Jolie deserves the world and she deserves a mother who is well. 

I went to a psychiatrist, one that was recommended by my dear uncle's good friend, basically begging to be fixed. And just like when I was 19, I was told I have GAD. And for the first time, I was prescribed SSRIs/anti-depressants. I asked the doctor if he could also just evaluate me for ADHD; I knew the symptoms and I checked almost every thing on the list. He made me take some tests, spoke to me about my life, asked me why I suspected ADHD, told me I'm just probably going through a bout of depression + anxiety, prescribed me some meds and sent me on my merry way.

10 days. 10 days I did not want to get out of bed. Every thing I read online told me that SSRIs take 6 weeks to take effect. And 10 days in I was hating it all. I hated that I didn't feel like playing with Jolie. I didn't even want to eat. The SSRIs are supposed to help with my anxiety. But here's the big ha-ha. The side effects of the medications is... ya guessed it... anxiety. I'm actually laughing right now thinking about how ridiculous it all is. 

But I'm not a quitter. I was convinced it would work... in six weeks. Though I did call my doctor to ask him if I could switch my dose to the morning vs. the night. I thought I might feel better that way. In the same conversation, he asked me if I could come in ASAP to talk about ADHD.

I think I saw him the very next day because I could not wait to be told there was a better "fix". 

That session went quick. He swapped out my medication for ADHD medication, told me it might 1 - 2 days for my body to regulate on the meds, but if my anxiety disappeared, then my anxiety is and always has been ADHD-based anxiety.

And it was like the clouds parted and the sun finally shone on my being because the first dose of the medication I took--and I will remember this forever--I saw the fucking light.

I spent 20 minutes on the floor with Jolie, uninterrupted, writing the number 3 with her over and over again. She's a leftie so she was writing it mirrored but we sat on the floor with one A4 sized paper and wrote the number 3 over and over again and for ONCE, FOR ONCE, my focus was just on her and that paper. My head did not think about what we needed to do next, nor did it think about what happened that week. For the first time I was actually, actively, consciously PRESENT. 

And I'm not delulu, I know nothing is an instant fix. But boy, did I start really honing in on utilising my clear, present mind.

For the rest of 2025, I'm sure my treatment plan saved me from entirely losing my mind. Because I did not overthink as much, and I had the stability and confidence to help all the people who are my dependents (be it legally, financially or emotionally). It was wonderful and I was steam train tugging and just bulldozing through any wall that was put up to slow me down.

But then the me-adjacent issues settled. And then there was me. 

I looked in the mirror one day and suddenly I am 10kg lighter (and not the "Wow! Yay! Hot Girl Summer 2k25!" kind). I lost 10kg in about 1.5 months. That could not have been healthy, but I don't know, I never went for checkup on that. Now that all the me-adjacent noise has quieted, I can suddenly hear myself, and nothing about myself felt familiar. Like, who am I? Who am I without all the noise? The noise kept me from really looking at myself, and maybe in some messed up way, that's what I wanted. I wanted to avoid addressing anything about myself.

I am now 1+ years in on my revelation of my brain. And now it that it's quiet again (more noise came up this year--and I tell you I have never been in so much ache and pain in my life), it feels as though I'm meeting myself again.

When people ask me, "What do you do?" so many people might think I have the glamorous answer of, "Everything." In recent times though, I've realised I'm quite embarrassed that I don't have a concrete answer that I can reply that question with confidence.

I have 0 confidence. Yea, I get it, my whole USP has always been I am the girl who does everything--that was literally the title of an article of me in CLEO magazine. But "Jane of all trades, master of none," is kind of a backhanded compliment, and I'm learning that I hate it. 

I said to a friend fairly recently, "many certifications, zero legacy," and joked with him that Zero Legacy would be the title of memoir. I get sad, and maybe a little jealous when I see my peers all have something concrete to show for, while I have little crumbs in different industries to present to the board. 

My self-esteem is shot (always has been) and my confidence is only fed by validation. And when I tell you my need for validation is crippling, I mean it is so crippling that I know everything I do, I do for praise. When have I ever done anything for myself? Even with projects I am proud of, ones that I feel feed my soul and fire me up, I will still need to show it to my select few and I wait like puppy, wagging my tail for a scratch behind the ear.

All the self-love I preached in my early 20s? I know I meant it, I know my intentions were to make sure the beautiful, wonderful people (who take their time to partake in my life virtually) get the source of love that should be the primary supplier, their own selves. But hellooo? I make those big, motivational claims and why was I really doing it? So I could get that ear scratch?! So that I'd get validation from other people?! How ironic that I preached self-love when it's coming to be obvious, I do not practice it myself.

I could boil it down to upbringing, or maybe my mother's choice of words as she raised me almost all on her own while I was growing up. But I am a full grown woman. I already know factually that my crippling desire for validation does come from my upbringing. But as a full grown woman, is it not my responsibility to make adjustments to how I feed myself emotionally?

I gotta stop waiting for people to feed me validation. I need to start _liking_ myself.

> I've always said that, when used in this context, "liking" me is--in this moment--more important than "loving me".
